**1. Summary**

Charles Dickens’ *A Tale of Two Cities* is set against the backdrop of the French Revolution, contrasting the social unrest in Paris with the relative stability of London. The narrative centers on Dr. Alexandre Manette, a physician who has been secretly imprisoned in the Bastille for eighteen years. His daughter, Lucie, whom he believed dead, is brought to him by Mr. Jarvis Lorry of Tellson’s Bank. Lucie’s love and devotion help restore Dr. Manette’s sanity, though he remains psychologically scarred, often reverting to the trauma of his imprisonment by making shoes.

In London, Lucie meets Charles Darnay, a French aristocrat who has renounced his title and family name (St. Evrémonde) due to his horror at his family’s cruelty. Darnay is falsely accused of treason but is acquitted thanks to the legal brilliance of Sydney Carton, a brilliant but alcoholic and disillusioned barrister who bears a striking physical resemblance to Darnay. Carton falls deeply in love with Lucie, who marries Darnay. Carton accepts his rejection with dignity, promising to do anything for Lucie and those she loves.

Meanwhile, in Paris, the revolutionary fervor intensifies. Ernest Defarge, a wine-shop keeper, and his wife, Madame Defarge, lead the Jacquerie. Madame Defarge knits a secret register of those condemned to die, driven by a personal vendetta against the Evrémonde family, who destroyed her own family. When Darnay returns to France to save his former servant, Gabelle, he is arrested as an emigrant and aristocrat. Despite Dr. Manette’s influence as a former Bastille prisoner, Darnay is condemned to death after Madame Defarge produces a letter written by Dr. Manette during his imprisonment. This letter denounces the Evrémonde brothers for their crimes against the Defarge family, revealing that Dr. Manette’s imprisonment was a result of his knowledge of these crimes.

Sydney Carton arrives in Paris and discovers that Madame Defarge intends to denounce Lucie and her father as well. To save the family, Carton devises a plan. He drugs Darnay, exchanges clothes with him, and has Darnay smuggled out of the prison in a carriage with Lucie, Dr. Manette, and Mr. Lorry. Carton takes Darnay’s place in the Conciergerie. He accompanies a young seamstress, who is also condemned, to the guillotine, offering her comfort in her final moments. Carton is executed, finding peace and purpose in his sacrifice. Madame Defarge, attempting to arrest Lucie, is killed in a struggle with Miss Pross, Lucie’s devoted servant. The novel ends with Carton’s prophetic vision of a future where the suffering of the Revolution is redeemed, and his name is honored by the family he saved.

**2. Brief Answers**

*   **Who marries Lucie?** Charles Darnay.
*   **Who goes to the guillotine in whose place?** Sydney Carton goes in Charles Darnay’s place.
*   **What motivates that sacrifice?** Carton’s unrequited love for Lucie and his desire to redeem his wasted life through a noble act.
*   **What happens to Madame Defarge?** She is killed in a struggle with Miss Pross; her own pistol discharges, killing her.
*   **What is Jerry Cruncher's secret occupation?** He is a "Resurrection-Man" (grave robber/body snatcher).
*   **What is Dr Manette's prison designation?** One Hundred and Five, North Tower.
*   **How is the Marquis connected to Darnay?** The Marquis St. Evrémonde is Darnay’s uncle.

**4. Passage and Explanation**

**Passage:**
> "It is a far, far better thing that I do, than I have ever done; it is a far, far better rest that I go to than I have ever known."

**Explanation:**
This final line encapsulates the novel’s central theme of redemption through sacrifice. Throughout the story, Sydney Carton is portrayed as a man who has squandered his talents and life in apathy and alcoholism. By taking Darnay’s place, he transforms his existence from one of "waste" to one of profound purpose. The quote supports the summary by confirming that Carton’s death is not a tragedy of defeat, but a triumphant spiritual victory, fulfilling his earlier promise to Lucie that he would "give his life, to keep a life you love beside you." It marks the completion of his character arc from a "jackal" to a hero.
